Recoverability
is not recognition.

A sequential frozen-language-model study of what sampling can expose, what hidden states can diagnose, and what inference-time selection still cannot reliably control.

The question

When a frozen model produces a correct trajectory somewhere in a sampled bank, can it recognize that trajectory well enough to deploy it?

Ekexistence→RXdiagnosis→SXcontrol

These are separate empirical propositions. A correct candidate can exist without a reliable selection rule; a hidden-state signal can diagnose correctness without furnishing a useful causal control direction.

Evidence, not a leaderboard claim

Three findings define the contribution.

01

Sampling can expose substantial headroom.

On the 400-task internal distribution, Base one-sample accuracy was 25.75%; its 32-sample pool oracle was 97.75%. The high-compute X5 selector reached 61.75%, above a 45.75% one-sample Instruct comparator.

Unequal inference compute: this is not a model-superiority comparison.

02

Diagnostic signal is not deployable control.

X8 found 17.19 percentage points of Base task-specific representation-selection headroom. Yet X7 causal steering, X8 nearest-experience selection, and X9 learned routing all failed their stronger frozen gates.

Diagnostic oracle views are analysis tools, not deployable verifiers.

03

Public transfer sets a sharp limit.

On all HumanEval+ tasks, Base ordered recoverability reached 18.29% at k=8; Instruct reached 80.49%. MetaAtlas selected 11/164 Base programs, and neither preregistered primary comparison was decisive.

Recognition cannot recover behavior absent from the candidate bank.

Sequential program

Every experiment remains in the record.

The X-labels are historical development identifiers, not a ranking of mechanisms.

  1. X5

    High-compute frozen selection

    Positive internally. A frozen Base system extracted more correct behavior than a one-sample Instruct comparator, at substantially greater inference compute.

  2. X6

    Coverage without recognition

    Negative. Near-oracle shortlist coverage did not produce reliable final selection.

  3. X7

    Single-pass spectral steering

    Negative. Proper and opposite intervention directions had identical aggregate accuracy.

  4. X8

    Metacognitive Atlas

    Mixed / negative. Task-dependent diagnostic headroom existed; adaptive nearest-experience routing failed against the best fixed view.

  5. X9

    Learned MetaRoute

    Negative. The 43,649-parameter router weakly predicted view utility and failed its preregistered gate.

  6. Public

    HumanEval+ transfer

    Not decisive. MetaAtlas did not decisively beat sample 0 or best fixed; Base recoverability remained far below Instruct.

Methods and safeguards

Measurement precedes interpretation.

Frozen protocol

Qwen2.5-7B Base and Instruct were evaluated in BF16 without quantization. Public transfer used one immutable eight-candidate bank per task, temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, and nested ordered prefixes.

Mechanical evaluation

Internal tasks use deterministic task-specific verifiers. HumanEval+ uses EvalPlus 0.3.1 on the full 164-task set. Selector outputs were written and hashed before evaluator outcomes were attached.

Paired inference

Primary selector comparisons use task-level paired bootstrap intervals (20,000 replicates) and exact two-sided McNemar tests. Failed gates leave confirmation sets untouched.

Metric correction

Ordered recoverability is not unbiased pass@k. Archived HumanEval+ unbiased pass@1, pass@2, and pass@4 are explicitly withdrawn; selected@8 and the primary paired tests are unaffected.

Limits and next tests

What this study does not establish.

  • It does not establish a general frozen-model control mechanism or a replacement for post-training.
  • Its internal benchmark is synthetic and private; it supports controlled comparisons, not public benchmark leadership.
  • The strongest X5 result uses unequal inference compute.
  • HumanEval+ is an aged benchmark; a preregistered, contamination-aware evaluation such as LiveCodeBench is the next appropriate test, without tuning this completed project on it.
  • The archived public run used EvalPlus's local evaluator rather than an isolated container; future reproductions should use official sandboxing or an equivalent isolated path.
